AI & Search

The AI Search Revolution is Here — And Most DMOs Aren't Ready

Google's Gemini 3.5 and OpenAI's GPT-5 now handle 40% of travel research queries. Yet only 31% of DMOs have adapted their content strategy for AI-generated answers. The shift from "ranking on page one" to "being cited in the AI answer" requires fundamental changes to how destinations create and structure content.

At Google I/O 2026 (May), the company announced that Gemini 3.5 would become the default search experience for travel queries. The AI now synthesizes information from multiple sources to provide direct answers rather than links. For DMOs, this means your content may inform the answer without ever receiving a click.

The emerging discipline of Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) or Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) focuses on structuring content to be cited by AI systems. Key principles include: clear factual statements, structured data markup, authoritative sourcing, and conversational Q&A formatting.

Early adopters are seeing results. Destinations that restructured their FAQ pages to match natural language queries saw a 45% increase in AI citation rates. Those using schema markup for events, attractions, and accommodations appeared in 3x more AI-generated itineraries.

Canadian Context: Destination Canada has begun piloting AEO strategies on their main tourism portal, restructuring content around question-based formats. Tourism Vancouver is testing structured data implementation across their events calendar. Both are measuring "AI mention rate" as a new KPI alongside traditional SEO metrics.

Considerations: This shift doesn't mean abandoning SEO — traditional search still drives the majority of traffic. The approach is additive: optimize for both traditional search and AI citation. Also note that AI systems learn from authoritative sources, so maintaining your destination's Wikipedia presence and Google Business listings remains critical.

DMO Implication: Audit your website content for AI-readability. Does each page answer a clear question? Is information structured in ways AI can parse? Start with your top 20 pages and restructure for both human readers and AI systems.

Seasonality

Canada's Shoulder Season is Having a Moment

Domestic adventure travel bookings for September-October are up 250% year-over-year. Air Canada projects fall 2026 as their strongest shoulder season on record, with load factors exceeding summer levels on key leisure routes. The combination of lower prices, fewer crowds, and perfect hiking weather is driving a structural shift in when Canadians travel.

Three factors are converging to supercharge shoulder season demand: post-pandemic flexibility in work arrangements allowing more off-peak travel, climate awareness making fall weather more appealing than summer heat, and a maturing domestic tourism market that increasingly values experience quality over peak-season status.

Adventure tourism is leading the shift. Guided hiking and cycling tours for September-October are selling out months in advance. Multi-day packages combining outdoor activities with food and wine experiences are the fastest-growing category. The demographic is expanding beyond traditional adventure travelers to include wellness-focused urban professionals.

Operators report that shoulder season guests spend 23% more per day than summer visitors, driven by willingness to upgrade accommodations and add premium experiences when overall trip costs are lower.

Canadian Context: British Columbia is seeing the strongest growth, with Tourism Kamloops reporting 180% increase in September inquiries. Atlantic Canada's shoulder season is extending later, with October now viable for outdoor programming. Quebec's Eastern Townships and Ontario's Prince Edward County are positioning as autumn culinary destinations.

Considerations: Shoulder season success requires operator coordination — travelers need assurance that restaurants, attractions, and experiences will be open. DMOs may need to facilitate extended-season agreements with key partners. Weather variability also means having indoor alternatives ready.

DMO Implication: Double down on shoulder season marketing now. The window to influence fall 2026 booking decisions is closing. Consider cooperative campaigns with operators willing to extend their seasons. Position your destination's specific autumn advantages: foliage timing, harvest events, wildlife viewing windows.

Climate Travel

The "Coolcation" Trend Positions Canada as Climate-Smart Destination

Searches for "cooler destinations" and "escape the heat" are up 74% globally. As European heat waves intensify — with record 45°C temperatures in Spain and Italy — travelers are reconsidering traditional summer destinations. Canada's temperate climate is becoming a genuine competitive advantage, not just a positioning angle.

The term "coolcation" emerged in 2024 travel media and has accelerated in 2026. It describes intentional travel to cooler climates during peak summer months, driven by both comfort preferences and climate awareness. The trend is particularly strong among travelers 35-55 who previously defaulted to Mediterranean beach destinations.

Google Trends data shows "summer vacation Canada" queries from UK and European IP addresses up 156% versus 2023. OTAs report 89% increase in transatlantic bookings to Canadian destinations from traditionally Europe-bound travelers.

This isn't just about temperature. Travelers are also seeking destinations perceived as climate-resilient, with lower wildfire risk and stable weather patterns. The 2023-2025 Canadian wildfire seasons created awareness challenges, but destinations in Atlantic Canada and parts of BC are positioning around their specific climate advantages.

Canadian Context: Newfoundland and Labrador is leaning into coolcation positioning with "20-degree summers" messaging. Tourism Nova Scotia is targeting UK travelers with direct comparisons to Scottish weather (with better food). Tourism Prince Edward Island reports European inquiries up 200% for summer 2027 planning.

Considerations: Coolcation messaging requires nuance. Leading with climate anxiety can feel negative; leading with comfort and natural beauty while climate functions as a secondary benefit works better. Also be prepared to address wildfire questions honestly — travelers are doing their research.

DMO Implication: If your destination offers genuine summer climate advantages, make this a core message for international marketing. Create content addressing the climate comparison directly. Consider partnerships with European travel media to place destination features during their heat wave news cycles.

Performance

Canadian Hotels Post Record August Performance

Canadian hotels achieved 78.9% occupancy and $267.44 ADR in August 2026, both record highs for the month. RevPAR reached $211.05, up 8.2% year-over-year. Montreal led major markets at 82% occupancy, driven by Jazz Festival momentum and strong international arrivals. The industry has definitively recovered from pandemic-era performance.

The August numbers cap a record-setting summer for Canadian hospitality. Year-to-date RevPAR is 12% above 2019 levels when adjusted for inflation, indicating genuine growth rather than just price recovery. International arrivals are driving the outperformance, up 24% versus 2019 for the January-August period.

Market-by-market performance varies significantly. Montreal's record results reflect successful destination marketing and event programming. Vancouver remains constrained by housing-related hotel conversion losses. Toronto benefits from business travel recovery plus strong leisure demand. Calgary and Edmonton are seeing energy-sector driven business travel return.

Luxury and upper-upscale segments continue outperforming, with ADR growth of 11% versus 6% for economy properties. The premiumization trend in travel spending persists despite inflation concerns.

Canadian Context: The strong performance supports DMO advocacy for continued tourism investment. Data showing record economic contribution strengthens the case for marketing budget increases and infrastructure development. Some markets are now discussing the need for new hotel supply to support continued growth.

Considerations: Record ADRs also mean affordability concerns for some traveler segments. Budget-conscious domestic travelers may be priced out of peak-season urban travel, creating opportunities for secondary destinations and shoulder-season positioning.

DMO Implication: Use strong performance data in stakeholder communications and budget requests. The narrative of tourism recovery is complete — the industry is now in growth mode. Position your destination's specific contribution to these national results.

Confidence

SourceSTR Canada, Hotel Association of Canada, CBRE Hotels

Sustainability

Visitor Dispersal: Turning Overtourism into Opportunity

80% of global tourists visit just 10% of destinations. The EU's 2026 Green Tourism Plan is actively incentivizing travel to lesser-known destinations, with carbon credits and booking platform partnerships steering travelers away from overcrowded hotspots. For Canadian secondary destinations, this creates a window to capture overflow and position as alternatives to congested competitors.

Barcelona, Venice, and Amsterdam have implemented visitor caps and tourist taxes that are pushing travelers to reconsider alternatives. Booking platforms are now featuring "lesser-known destinations" prominently, with Airbnb's "Icons" program specifically highlighting emerging destinations. The infrastructure for visitor dispersal is being built into the booking funnel.

Secondary destinations benefit when they can offer comparable experiences without the crowds. The positioning strategy is "you came for X, stay for Y" — acknowledging the gateway attraction while redirecting to less-visited areas for the actual trip.

Technology is enabling more sophisticated dispersal. Real-time crowding data, timed-entry systems, and AI-powered itinerary tools that route around peak congestion are becoming standard. Destinations that implement these tools can accommodate more visitors while improving experience quality.

Canadian Context: Banff and Jasper are testing timed-entry and parking reservation systems that could become models for other congested Canadian destinations. Tourism Whistler's village management program is being studied by other mountain communities. Meanwhile, destinations like Revelstoke, Fernie, and the Kootenays are positioning as alternatives that offer comparable mountain experiences without the crowds.

Considerations: Dispersal requires coordination across multiple DMOs and with transportation providers. A visitor can't easily choose a secondary destination if getting there requires significantly more time or cost. Regional collaboration and transit investment are preconditions for successful dispersal.

DMO Implication: If you're a secondary destination near a congested hotspot, build explicit "alternative" positioning. Create content that addresses the comparison directly. If you're a primary destination facing crowding, implement visitor management technology before being forced to by regulation. Both strategies require proactive planning.

SourceUNWTO, European Commission Green Tourism Plan, Airbnb Trends Report
