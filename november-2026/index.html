Travel Costs Running at 2x Inflation

Economics

Travel prices are surging far ahead of the broader economy. The U.S. Travel Price Index is up 8.7% YoY versus just 3.4% for overall CPI. This is changing how travelers book, but not whether they travel.

September 2026 Data

The TPI rose 2.1% in September alone, its largest monthly gain since March 2026. Airfares are up 25.5% YoY. Gasoline rose 27.4% YoY. Travel price growth is more than doubling the broader CPI.

Higher costs are changing how Americans travel, not whether they travel. Air trips are expected to decline 0.8% in 2026, while car/other mode trips rise 1.9%. 52% say they'll still travel but adjust plans. Only 1% are postponing travel entirely. The winners will be destinations accessible by car within drive markets.

Sources: U.S. Travel Association TPI, Skift State of Travel 2026, Bureau of Labor Statistics

Affluent Travelers Carrying the Industry

Economics

The travel industry is bifurcating. Revenue growth is outpacing volume growth by 2:1 as affluent travelers drive gains. OTA Q3 earnings reveal the pattern: room nights up 7%, but gross bookings up 10-12%.

Q3 2026 OTA Earnings

Booking Holdings: Room nights +7%, gross bookings +10%. Expedia: Room nights +7%, revenue +12%. Airbnb: ADR +4-6% across regions. American Express: T&E spending +10%, airline category +13%.

McKinsey reports luxury travel growing at 6% CAGR, outpacing broader travel by 2 percentage points. BCG found top-tier luxury clients (€50K+ annual spend) increased their share of total luxury spend from 14% to 24%. Skift notes "revenue growth is increasingly carried by higher prices and a smaller group of affluent travelers rather than by rising volumes." The Skift Travel Health Index stood at 100 in August, indicating flat YoY growth in volume.

AI Planning Hits Inflection Point

AI

AI adoption for travel planning has crossed the chasm. 56% of U.S. leisure travelers used AI for at least one trip in 2026, up from 43% just nine months earlier. PhocusWright calls it "the fastest behavioral shift the travel industry has seen in more than a decade."

The Trust Gap

91% use AI as a trip planning tool, but only 2% trust AI to actually book without human oversight. Primary uses: recommendations (75%), itinerary planning (70%), discovery (69%). Only 13% expect to use AI for booking.

Deloitte tracked GenAI use for trip planning rising from 15% (2025) to 25% (2026). High-income millennials lead at 43% adoption. Simon-Kucher found nearly two-thirds of travelers under 45 would use AI for recommendations, with adoption tapering to 44% for Gen X and 29% for boomers. The opportunity is in the handoff from planning to booking.

Sources: PhocusWright Travel Forward 2026, Deloitte 2026 Travel Outlook, Klook Travel Pulse 2026

OTAs Prove AI Users Convert at 2x Rate

AI

The conversion data is in. Booking Holdings reports AI assistant users convert at 2x the rate of non-users and have average booking values ~20% higher. This is no longer speculation; it's measurable business impact.

Q3 2026 Earnings Call

AI assistant users at Priceline and Agoda convert at about twice the rate of non-users. Agoda AI users have average booking values approximately 20% higher. The company "continues making progress integrating AI across its brands."

Google is working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott, IHG, Choice, and Wyndham to bring hotel bookings directly into AI Mode. 64% of DMOs now write content specifically formatted for AI engines. 51% are either very concerned about generative AI's impact or actively developing a response strategy. The question isn't whether to optimize for AI. It's how fast you can adapt.

Global Outlook Diverges by Region

Performance

International tourism is in a holding pattern. H1 2026 growth was just 0.4%, the slowest since recovery began, with 690 million arrivals. But 2027 is forecast to accelerate to +8% as geopolitical pressures ease.

UNWTO September Barometer

Full-year 2026 outlook revised down from 3-4% growth to 1-2%. Middle East conflict and elevated oil prices cited as primary drags. 1.57 billion international arrivals expected, but 2027 forecast jumps to +8%.

Regional performance varies dramatically. Europe leads at +5.6%, Africa at +4%, Americas at +2%. Asia-Pacific remains 11% below 2019 levels. The Middle East dropped 7.5% in September but is forecast to rebound +43% in 2027. Canada saw Q1 domestic trips +1.5% (78.7M trips) and overseas visitor spending +10.2% to $2.1B. WTTC forecasts $12 trillion global Travel & Tourism GDP in 2026.

H1 2026 growth +0.4%
2027 forecast growth +8%
Europe growth +5.6%
Global T&T GDP $12 trillion
Canada overseas spending +10.2%

Sources: UNWTO World Tourism Barometer, WTTC Economic Impact Research, Statistics Canada

The B2B Pivot Is Real

Business

The next growth wave for travel platforms isn't consumer acquisition. It's powering other businesses. Expedia B2B bookings grew 19% vs B2C at just 7% in Q3 2026. B2B revenue growth of 23% "showcases durability and high demand."

Expedia Q3 2026 Earnings

B2B gross bookings grew 19%, B2C grew just 7%. Strategy: Becoming a "one-stop travel shop" for partners. Recent acquisition of CarTrawler and launch of new activities APIs expand the platform play.

The industry-wide shift is clear: OTA bookings generated $408 billion in 2026 (PhocusWright). Online gross bookings reached $1.2 trillion by end of 2026. 65% of global travel bookings now made online. Tours and activities are only 33% online booked vs 64% for travel overall, leaving massive runway for digitization. The experiences market is projected to reach $342 billion by 2029.

Recommended Actions

Six strategic moves for DMO leaders responding to the price-value paradox, AI disruption, and the road to 2027. Each action includes budget-appropriate approaches.

01

Optimize for the affluent traveler

The Evidence

Revenue growth is outpacing volume growth by 2:1. OTA Q3 earnings show room nights up 7%, but gross bookings up 10-12%. McKinsey reports luxury travel growing at 6% CAGR, outpacing broader travel by 2 percentage points. The market is bifurcating: affluent travelers are carrying the industry.

What Changes In Your Week

$50K+ budget: Develop tiered experiences and premium positioning. Partner with operators on value-add packages rather than discounts. Under $50K: Pull your top 20% of guests by spend and analyze their behavior. Consider repositioning from "affordable" to "quality and unique." The market supports premium pricing.

EconomicsStrategy
02

Make your content AI-legible

The Evidence

64% of DMOs now write content specifically formatted for AI engines. 56% of U.S. travelers used AI for trips in 2026. Google is working with major travel brands to bring bookings directly into AI Mode. AI Overviews appear on 50%+ of travel-related Google searches.

What Changes In Your Week

Any budget: Run the AI audit. Query ChatGPT and Perplexity with "[your destination] vacation planning." Is the response accurate, helpful, and bookable? Content strategy: Structure content with clear answers to common questions. AI engines reward specificity over marketing language.

AIStrategy
03

Build the AI-to-booking handoff

The Evidence

91% use AI to plan, only 2% trust AI to actually book. But AI users convert at 2x the rate of non-users when they do book. The trust gap is the opportunity. Travelers want AI discovery followed by human confirmation.

What Changes In Your Week

$50K+ budget: Ensure your booking flow accepts AI-discovered leads seamlessly. Consider chat or human concierge options for high-value bookings. Under $50K: Make your contact information and booking links prominent. The AI sends them to you, so make sure they can convert with a human touch.

AIDemand
04

Re-evaluate your channel mix

The Evidence

Display advertising dropped from 75% to 45% of DMO spend. TikTok adoption fell from 49% to 28%. YouTube grew to 55%. Google travel searches are down ~10% throughout 2026 even as travel volume holds steady. AI may be capturing discovery.

What Changes In Your Week

$50K+ budget: Audit your channel mix against attribution data. If you can't prove TikTok ROI, reallocate to YouTube, connected TV, or owned channels. Under $50K: Focus on channels you can measure. Prioritize platforms where performance attribution is clear.

Paid MediaPerformance
05

Prepare for 2027 acceleration

The Evidence

2026 is a holding year: H1 growth was just 0.4%. But 2027 international arrivals are forecast to grow +8% (vs +2% in 2026). Asia-Pacific and Middle East recovery will drive demand. Europe continues leading at +5.6%. The rebound is coming.

What Changes In Your Week

Any budget: If you're planning campaigns for 2027, build in capacity for the rebound. Watch the 2027 forecasts closely. International markets: Consider re-engagement with Asia-Pacific and Middle East source markets as geopolitical pressures ease.

Performance2027 Planning
06

Consider B2B partnerships

The Evidence

Expedia B2B is growing 3x faster than B2C (+19% vs +7%). Tours and activities are only 33% online booked vs 64% for travel overall, leaving massive runway for digitization. The next growth wave for travel platforms isn't consumer acquisition. It's powering other businesses.

What Changes In Your Week

$50K+ budget: Review your distribution strategy. Platform partnerships may offer more efficient reach than direct-to-consumer campaigns. Explore B2B integrations for experiences and activities. Any budget: Ensure your operators are properly listed on platform partners. Your tech stack partner matters more than ever.
