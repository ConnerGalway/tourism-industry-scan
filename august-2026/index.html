Key Trends

Five marketing insights shaping Canadian destination marketing this month, synthesized from eight industry sources.

Marketing

Short-Form Video is the New Discovery Channel

70% of Gen Z and Millennials now find their next destination through short-form video. A 15-second behind-the-scenes reel outperformed a professionally shot property tour by 340% on reach. TikTok Travel Ads launched in late 2025, making the platform a full-funnel conversion tool.

In June we discussed set-jetting — travelers inspired by screen content. This month we're seeing the channel shift that enables it. TikTok's data shows 60% of users visit new destinations after discovering them on the platform. 59% of UK users who book after TikTok discovery do so within one week.

Short-form video drives 2.8x more engagement than static travel ads. YouTube Shorts achieves 5.91% engagement — the highest of all platforms. The brands seeing results prioritize authenticity over production value.

TikTok's new Travel Ads product, powered by the Smart+ AI engine, launched globally in September 2025 with three catalogue types: Hotels, Flights, and Destinations. It's now a full-funnel tool, not just awareness.

DMO Implication: Reallocate production budget from polished long-form content to high-volume authentic short-form. The data is clear: travelers respond to real moments, not commercials.

Confidence

SourceTikTok Business Insights, Miles Partnership, Dash Social

Strategy

DMOs Shift from Brand to Performance

In 2025, 59% of DMOs globally said top-of-funnel awareness was their primary focus. In 2026, that dropped to 25% — a 34-point shift in one year. Nearly 80% of North American DMOs now say ROI and economic impact are their top reporting priorities.

This isn't a gradual trend — it's a strategic pivot driven by tightening budgets, stakeholder pressure, and a performance-first mandate. Conversions as a main campaign goal jumped from 15% to 31% year-over-year.

47% of DMOs are now running full-funnel campaigns that guide travelers from awareness through to booking. In North America, 79% of DMOs prioritize hotel room nights and direct revenue as key metrics.

The DMOs performing best aren't choosing between brand and performance — they're running both. Digital spending is holding steady: 41% of DMOs increased digital spending in 2026, 41% held flat, only 13% cut back.

DMO Implication: If your marketing plan still separates "awareness" and "conversion" into different budgets with different teams, you're working against the industry direction. Connect awareness spending to booking outcomes.

Confidence

SourceSojern State of Destination Marketing 2026

Marketing

Micro-Influencers Outperform Every Paid Channel

Micro-influencers (10K-250K followers) generate $5-6.50 for every dollar spent — outperforming macro-influencers, traditional paid social, and display advertising. 82% of travelers trust creator recommendations over traditional ads. Booking conversion: 3-7% vs 0.5-1% for display.

Engagement rates tell the story: micro-influencers achieve 6.15-6.76% engagement versus just 1-2% for larger accounts. 73% of travelers say influencer recommendations have directly led to a booking, rising to 83% among under-40s.

92% of DMOs are increasing or maintaining their influencer marketing budget in 2026. The most successful programs have moved past transactional campaign-by-campaign relationships to long-term partnerships with creators genuinely connected to the destination.

Long-form travelogues combined with Reels is the content combination delivering the strongest ROI. Creators who have built authentic associations with a destination over multiple visits generate more credible content.

DMO Implication: If you're running one-off influencer trips, you're leaving most of the value on the table. Build long-term relationships with creators who genuinely connect with your destination.

Confidence

SourceEvok Destination Influencer Marketing Guide 2026, Steller Report

Case Study

Montreal's Marketing Playbook: Reality TV Campaign Drives Record Results

Tourisme Montreal's summer campaign, developed with Cossette, positions the city as a reality TV stage where visitors become stars. The "one island, six challengers" creative drove the city to 12.5 million visitors (record), 82% hotel occupancy, and record Jazz Festival attendance.

The 60-second hero video uses reality-TV tropes to position Montreal as a living set filled with cultural and gastronomic adventures. Key activation: Penn Station in New York, targeting American commuters.

As CMO Sylvie Charette explained, the campaign "highlights the city's wealth of discoveries, meaningful encounters, and intense moments." It's bold, differentiated, and ownable.

CEO Yves Lalumière's positioning is equally sharp: "Toronto is the downtown of Canada. Montreal is the playground of Canada." That clarity — knowing exactly what you are and aren't — is what great destination marketing looks like.

DMO Implication: Study Montreal's approach: bold creative, clear positioning, strategic activation in high-traffic locations. They didn't try to be everything. They owned "playground" — and it's working.

Confidence

SourceTourisme Montreal, Strategy Magazine, Canadian Press

Operations

The Response Gap: 94% of Social Messages Go Unanswered

Travel brands respond to only 6% of incoming social messages. That means 94% of interactions — many from high-intent travelers actively considering your destination — go completely unanswered. Meanwhile, 64% of consumers are comfortable booking directly through social platforms.

The disconnect is stark: 73% of travelers say influencer recommendations have led to a booking. 87% of Gen Z use social media to research trips. The intent is there. The infrastructure to convert is there. The basic responsiveness isn't.

Best practice is simple: reply to every comment and DM within 24 hours. Yet most travel brands are nowhere close. This is the lowest-hanging fruit in destination marketing.

The majority of operators say getting more direct bookings is their top priority, but less than one in four rate their marketing as effective. Before optimizing paid strategy or launching another influencer campaign, audit your response rates.

DMO Implication: Before you launch another campaign, answer the messages you already have. If someone asks about your destination and gets silence, you've lost them. Set a 24-hour response target for all social interactions.

SourceVamoos Social Media Marketing Guide 2026, CuFinder Tourism Benchmarks
