## Slide 3: Trend 1 — World Cup: A Cautionary Tale

**Visual:** Canada map with Toronto and Vancouver highlighted. Contrasting callout bubbles: Vancouver showing +21.3% ADR alongside -15.8% occupancy. Progress tracker pill bar shows "1" filled. Consider a "pricing vs. volume" visual tension.

**Content blocks:**
- **International arrivals:** +5% YoY in June, well below projections
- **Vancouver:** ADR +21.3% to CAD$406, but occupancy -15.8% to 73.2%
- **Toronto:** ADR +19.0%, similar rate-over-volume pattern
- **The diagnosis:** Businesses inflated prices early and reduced promotional efforts, assuming demand would come automatically

**Conner:**
> "Let's talk about the elephant in the room. Canada hosted 13 World Cup matches with over a billion dollars in investment. International arrivals rose just 5 percent. Look at Vancouver: ADR jumped 21 percent, but occupancy dropped nearly 16 percent. The pattern tells a story. The industry assumed mega-event demand was guaranteed, so operators raised prices aggressively and cut back on promotion. That pricing posture may have suppressed the very demand we were counting on. This isn't about blaming anyone. It's about learning before the next major event."

**Pause point:** "Mega-events require active promotion, not complacency."

**Source:** TravelMole, CoStar/STR, BNN Bloomberg

## Key Narrative Throughline

This month's scan centers on a question: **Did we get in our own way?**

The World Cup data suggests that complacency — assuming demand would materialize without active promotion, and pricing aggressively without regard to volume — may have suppressed the very arrivals we were counting on. Meanwhile, destinations that executed well on smaller, unique events captured outsized returns.

The lesson isn't that mega-events don't work. It's that no event sells itself. Active marketing, coordinated pricing, and real-time responsiveness matter more than the size of the occasion.

This narrative should inform the tone throughout: constructive, forward-looking, focused on actionable lessons rather than finger-pointing.
