Key Trends

Changes in Canadian tourism this month, synthesized from ten industry sources. Focus: industry economics, workforce, and technology.

Domestic Tourism Boom

Demand

Canadians are overwhelmingly choosing to travel at home. 70% of Canadians are avoiding US travel in 2026, with 92% planning at least one domestic trip. This is the biggest domestic travel boom in a generation.

Reshored Spending

$4.4 billion in reshored Canadian spending projected between 2025-2027. Despite domestic flight prices up 36% YoY (avg $410 vs $294), Canadians continue choosing home destinations.

A BDC survey found 92% of Canadian travelers planned at least one domestic trip in 2026. 67% cite political climate and US-Canada tensions. 59% no longer feel safe traveling to the US. Q1 2026 saw 69.1 million domestic trips (+2.3% YoY) with $14.5B in spending (+5.1%).

Record Q3 2026 Performance

Economics

Canada's visitor economy had its strongest quarter yet in Q3 2026. 3.1 million visitors arrived in July alone, with visitor spending on track for $140.9 billion this year—up 6% from 2025.

August Performance

Overseas arrivals surged to 760,464 (+7.2% YoY). Air arrivals reached 670,100 (+9.9%). Year-to-date international overnight arrivals hit 11.7 million through July.

Key source markets driving growth include the UK, Mexico, and France, with Mexico showing dramatic year-over-year volume increases. Q1 2026 saw international visitors make 4.5 million trips with spending up 13.8% to $5.0 billion. By 2035, total tourism revenue is forecast to reach $216.3 billion—a 67% increase over 2024 levels.

Canadians Go Global Again

Consumer

After a summer of staying home, Canadians are widening their horizons this fall. Only 61% plan to travel domestically this fall—down sharply from 77% in summer. International destinations are surging back.

The Breakout Destinations

Casablanca, Morocco saw bookings soar +250% YoY. Liberia, Costa Rica jumped +145%. Aruba rose +118%. Delhi overtook Tokyo as the #1 international city destination for Canadians in 2026. The world is calling again.

9% of fall travelers plan to visit Mexico (up from 4% in summer). 23% are heading to other international destinations (up from 15%). Trips to the US dropped 10.6% in Q1 2026, while overseas visits rose 6.2%. Mexico captured 1.3 million Canadian visits in Q1 alone. France and Japan are strengthening as long-haul alternatives.

The Trade War Tourism Reversal

Economics

A rare tourism reversal is underway. While Canadians boycott the US, Americans are flooding north to capitalize on a C$1.37 exchange rate. The trade war is reshaping cross-border travel in both directions.

The Cross-Border Flip

Canadian visits to the US fell 25% in 2025 vs 2023-24 levels. That drop cost American border communities an estimated 14,000-42,000 jobs. Meanwhile, US-to-Canada travel is surging as Americans treat Canadian cities as high-value getaways.

60% of Canadians say they're less open to US travel than at any point in the past year. Cross-border travel spiked 6.8% in September as Canadians made 1.9 million return trips from the US. Rather than cutting back, Canadians are redirecting—69.1 million domestic journeys and $14.5 billion in internal spending in Q1 2026. The diplomatic standoff shows no signs of resolution.

Hotel Rate-Driven Growth Continues

Performance

Canada's hotel industry reported its eighth consecutive month of year-over-year growth in ADR and RevPAR as of August 2026. The industry is achieving growth primarily through rate increases rather than occupancy gains.

August 2026 Metrics

National occupancy: 80.9% (+0.2%). ADR: CAD 267.48 (+6.7%). RevPAR: CAD 216.31 (+6.9%). Toronto led with ADR +10% to CAD 308 and RevPAR +15.1%.

Quebec reported highest occupancy lift (+3.2% to 83.7%). Nova Scotia posted largest ADR increase (+9.0% to CAD 289.67). June 2026 marked the first monthly occupancy decline since December 2025, signaling potential softening—though summer rebounded strongly.

Booking.com's 2027 Travel Predictions

Consumer

Booking.com's annual predictions reveal that 2027 travelers will go "completely out of character"—69% of Canadian travelers want vacations that surprise friends and family. Travel is increasingly about transformation and breaking patterns.

Relationship Travel

39% of Canadian couples would take a trip to resolve conflict. 67% say vacation activities help difficult conversations. Top method: hiking together (33%). 51% of solo travelers say physical challenges led to lasting friendships.

Long-haul travel barriers are falling: 88% would take a long journey if they'd arrive rested. Travelers want sleep-supportive menus (33%), jet-lag-reducing lighting (26%), and skincare kits (17%). 44% would join communities of like-minded athletes while traveling.

Recommended Actions

Six strategic moves for DMO leaders responding to the domestic tourism boom, AI disruption, and the road to 2027. Each action includes budget-appropriate approaches.

01

Capitalize on the domestic travel boom with targeted campaigns

The Evidence

70% of Canadians are avoiding US travel in 2026. 92% plan at least one domestic trip. $4.4 billion in reshored spending is projected through 2027. This is the biggest opportunity for domestic destinations in a generation.

What Changes In Your Week

Provincial DMOs: Shift paid media to domestic markets. Target Canadians who previously vacationed in the US—especially California, Florida, Arizona travelers. Regional DMOs: Create "Why fly south?" campaigns positioning your winter offerings. Any budget: Update messaging to celebrate Canadian travel pride. The sentiment is real—lean into it.

DemandStrategy
02

Prepare for outbound competition as Canadians go global

The Evidence

Only 61% of fall travelers plan to stay domestic—down from 77% in summer. Mexico bookings jumped from 4% to 9%. Casablanca surged +250%, Costa Rica +145%. The summer's domestic surge is softening as Canadians look abroad again.

What Changes In Your Week

Winter destinations: You're now competing with Mexico and Morocco, not just Florida. Emphasize unique Canadian winter experiences that can't be replicated. All DMOs: The "stay home" momentum is fading—you'll need to earn domestic travelers again. Any budget: Position your destination as the exotic alternative within Canada. If Casablanca can surge 250%, what's your "discovery" angle?

03

Capture American visitors fleeing to Canada

The Evidence

The USD at ~C$1.37 makes Canada a high-value destination for Americans. While Canadian visits to the US fell 25%, US-to-Canada travel is surging. Americans are treating Canadian provincial cities, national parks, and maritime harbors as premium getaways.

What Changes In Your Week

Border regions: Target US markets within driving distance with "your dollar goes further" messaging. Urban DMOs: Position your city as the sophisticated alternative—Americans can experience world-class dining, culture, and nightlife at a 27% discount. Any budget: Run paid campaigns in US border states. The exchange rate does the selling for you.

04

Leverage rate-driven growth for premium positioning

The Evidence

Canada's hotel industry marked its eighth consecutive month of RevPAR growth. ADR reached CAD 267.48 (+6.7%). Toronto led with ADR +10% to CAD 308. Growth is coming from rate increases, not occupancy gains—the market supports premium pricing.

What Changes In Your Week

$50K+ budget: Partner with operators on value-add packages rather than discounts. The market supports premium pricing—don't train visitors to wait for deals. Under $50K: Audit your promotional messaging. If you're still leading with "affordable" or "budget-friendly," consider repositioning toward quality and unique experiences. Rate tolerance is high.

PerformanceRevenue
05

Target "out of character" travelers for 2027

The Evidence

69% of Canadian travelers want vacations that are "completely out of character" for 2027. 39% of couples would take a trip to resolve conflict. 88% would take long-haul journeys if they'd arrive rested. Travel is increasingly about transformation.

What Changes In Your Week

Any budget: Position your destination as a place for personal transformation, not just relaxation. Feature experiences that challenge visitors to try something new. Content strategy: Show people doing things they'd never do at home. The "try something unexpected" angle resonates with 2027's travel mindset.

Consumer2027 Planning
06

Monitor TikTok ROI and reallocate to measured channels

The Evidence

TikTok adoption among DMOs has dropped from 49% to just 28% year-on-year. Display advertising fell from 75% to 45%. DMOs are prioritizing platforms where performance can be clearly measured and attributed.

What Changes In Your Week

$50K+ budget: Audit your channel mix against attribution data. If you can't prove TikTok ROI, reallocate to search, connected TV, or owned channels. Under $50K: Focus on channels you can measure. If VisitScotland can go viral on TikTok with existing footage and zero spend, creative execution matters more than budget. But if you're not measuring, you're guessing.
