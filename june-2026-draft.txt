TREND 1: World Cup Revenue Surge Hits Canadian Host Cities
Tag: Economics
Confidence: High

Canadian hotels in FIFA World Cup host cities are capturing significant premiums.
Toronto saw ADR lift 47.6% and RevPAR increase 36.2% around its June 12 match.
Vancouver recorded even stronger gains with ADR jumping 54.0% and RevPAR up 30.4%
for its June 13 fixture. However, Vancouver's booking status as of June 15 showed
occupancy at 64% for the June 24 match, down 8.6% year-over-year, suggesting
demand may be softer for later-stage games or pricing is pushing some travelers
to alternatives.

DMO Implication: Host city DMOs should be documenting these premiums for future
mega-event bids. Non-host destinations within driving distance have an opportunity
to position as value alternatives.

Source: STR via Hospitality Net, June 2026

TREND 2: Canadian Grand Prix Calendar Shift Reshapes Quebec Performance
Tag: Economics
Confidence: High

The Canadian Grand Prix's move from June to May dramatically boosted Quebec

DMO Implication: Major event calendar changes create significant revenue shifts.

TREND 4: Sustainable Procurement Becomes Mandatory - And It Affects Canadian Exports
Tag: Sustainability
Confidence: High

Regulatory pressure is forcing hospitality supply chain transformation. The EU's
Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) now requires in-scope
companies to report on supply chain environmental impact. The Corporate
Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) mandates identification of adverse
environmental and human rights impacts within supplier networks.

France's AGEC Law restricts single-use plastics in hospitality amenities and key
cards. Singapore's Hotel Sustainability Roadmap targets emission and waste
reductions by 2030. Travel surveys from 2023-2024 show sustainability credentials
are increasingly cited as differentiators, particularly among guests aged 25-44.

Canadian operators looking to export through travel trade should pay close
attention: travelers arriving from markets where these standards are mandatory
will expect similar sustainability practices at Canadian properties. Their travel
trade partners - tour operators, OTAs, and travel agencies in Europe and Asia -
are increasingly required to verify supplier credentials as part of their own
compliance obligations. A Canadian hotel or experience provider without
documentation may find itself excluded from international distribution channels.

DMO Implication: This is no longer just about guest preferences - it's about
market access. Supporting operator members in building documentation and
verification capabilities is now a competitive necessity for inbound tourism.

Source: Hospitality Net, June 2026

TREND 5: Responsible Tourism Innovation in Mountain Destinations
Tag: Sustainability
Confidence: Medium

Banff & Lake Louise Tourism is pioneering visitor management and sustainability
programs that offer models for other destinations. "Park Mode" encourages
responsible visitor behavior through activation events and rewards. "Trip Builder"
is a digital planning tool that manages expectations and distributes visitor
traffic more effectively. "Cool 2 Carpool" at Mt. Norquay offers deeply discounted
lift tickets to visitors who share rides.

Results include: Pursuit Hotels invested nearly $350,000 in energy efficiency
projects; Banff Borrows diverted 37,357 single-use items from waste at the 2025
Christmas Market; partnerships with Stoney Nakoda First Nations create Indigenous
tourism career pathways.

DMO Implication: Visitor management tools and incentive programs can shift
behavior at scale. DMOs facing capacity pressure should study the Banff model.

Source: Banff Lake Louise Tourism
